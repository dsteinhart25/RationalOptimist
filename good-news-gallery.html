The World Is Getting Better

The data tells a story the news doesn't. Hover over any chart to explore the numbers behind humanity's remarkable progress.

Extreme Poverty Is Disappearing

-75%
decline since 1990

Extreme poverty has plummeted from afflicting over a third of humanity to under 10%. Hundreds of millions of people have been lifted into better lives — the greatest humanitarian achievement in history.

Source: World Bank · International poverty line ($2.15/day, 2017 PPP)

Children Are Surviving Like Never Before

-61%
decline since 1990

In 1990, 1 in 11 children died before their fifth birthday. Today it's 1 in 27. Vaccines, clean water, and better nutrition are saving millions of young lives every year.

Source: UNICEF / UN IGME · Deaths per 1,000 live births, under-5

America's Air Keeps Getting Cleaner

-78%
emissions drop since 1970

Since 1970, the US economy tripled in size, vehicle miles doubled, and population grew 63% — yet total emissions of the six major air pollutants dropped 78%. Innovation and smart regulation work.

Source: US EPA · Aggregate emissions index (1970 = 100)

US Carbon Emissions: Down 18% From Peak

-18%
total CO₂ since 2007 peak

America emits 18% less total carbon than at its 2007 peak — not per capita, total. The shift from coal to natural gas and renewables is driving this remarkable decoupling of economic growth from emissions.

Source: U.S. Energy Information Administration · Billion metric tons CO₂

Famines Are Going Extinct

-94%
famine death rate since 1920s

Thanks to innovation that's led to an abundance of food, combined with trade between countries that quickly gets food to where it's needed most, famines are going extinct. The famine death rate has plummeted from hundreds per 100k to near zero.

Source: Our World in Data / World Peace Foundation · Deaths per 100,000 world population (decade average)

Air Pollution Death Rate: Halved Since 1990

-50%
death rate since 1990

While total deaths remain high due to population growth, the death rate from air pollution — the risk for the average person — has halved since 1990. As countries grow wealthier, they clean up their air.

Source: Global Burden of Disease / State of Global Air · Age-standardized death rate per 100,000
